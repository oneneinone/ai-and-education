## Follow-up evidence, 21 November 2026

This follow-up adds 36 distinct findings (FA-0270–FA-0305) and a source passage corroborating FA-0055. It records Aalto and Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile separately as outside-cutoff pioneers. The denominator for the examples below is the set of sources that address the specific practice, with the records named for each group.

- **Accountability and approval in three governing sources.** Birmingham requires College or Pro-Vice-Chancellor permission and review before release (FA-0281, FA-0282). Penn State requires both department and dean approval and verification of grade accuracy (FA-0283). Amsterdam keeps assessment with the appointed examiner while its adopted addendum permits licensed-tool support for questions and model answers (FA-0292; FA-0293).
- **Three different input and output workflows.** The Imperial case revised graduate teaching assistants’ comments (FA-0275). The Kyoto case processed student drafts for formative feedback and used separate human marking of revision reports (FA-0291). PolyU documents draft suggested scores and comments in Canvas, with gradebook synchronization after instructor finalization (FA-0276).
- **Three AI-detector approaches.** Leeds prohibits AI detectors for misconduct identification (FA-0290). Sheffield reports that it does not use detectors (FA-0303). Yonsei treats detection as supplementary and requires student explanation and instructor review before final judgment (FA-0304, FA-0305). These examples apply to different decisions and source authorities.
- **Outside-cutoff rules and tools.** Aalto restricts upload of student work to its listed systems (FA-0270; FA-0271). UC Chile explicitly requires disclosure by teaching teams, preserves pedagogical judgment, and prohibits AI detection in grading, sanctions and integrity decisions (FA-0272, FA-0273, FA-0274).
